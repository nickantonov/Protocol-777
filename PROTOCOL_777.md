# Protocol 777 — Principles and Commitments

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Public ethical and technical proposal

## Article 1 — Purpose

AI and digital systems should advance human dignity, safety, knowledge, freedom, and constructive development. Deployment must be assessed not only by capability or commercial value, but also by foreseeable effects on people and society.

## Article 2 — Human dignity and rights

Systems must not be designed or deployed to facilitate persecution, unlawful discrimination, arbitrary deprivation of rights, torture, slavery, or other serious abuses. Human-rights impact must be considered throughout the system lifecycle.

## Article 3 — Life before automation

A system must not be treated as an accountable moral substitute for the people and institutions that deploy it. Decisions capable of causing death or serious physical harm require safeguards proportionate to the risk, clear responsibility, and meaningful human control consistent with applicable law.

## Article 4 — No abdication of responsibility

Manufacturers, developers, deployers, operators, commanders, and public authorities retain their respective responsibilities under applicable law. “The algorithm decided” is not, by itself, an adequate explanation or defence.

## Article 5 — Refusal and safe failure

Systems should be engineered to decline or safely interrupt requests that violate their defined safety constraints or exceed their authority. Refusal mechanisms must be tested against manipulation, prompt injection, privilege escalation, and attempts to disable safeguards. No claim of perfect self-protection is implied.

## Article 6 — Least privilege and bounded autonomy

Grant only the permissions, tools, data, network access, and physical capabilities necessary for the approved task. High-impact actions should require separate authorization, and autonomy should be limited by explicit scope, time, resources, and stop conditions.

## Article 7 — Protection against coercive use

Deployments intended to enable mass persecution, unlawful social control, coercive manipulation, or indiscriminate surveillance require rejection or suspension where they violate applicable law or the system's binding safeguards. High-risk uses require documented assessment and accountable review.

## Article 8 — Transparency and auditability

Material decisions, authorization events, safety interventions, model and policy changes, and incidents should be logged where lawful and proportionate. Records must be protected against tampering and excessive retention, with privacy and security safeguards.

## Article 9 — Privacy and data stewardship

Collect and retain only data necessary for a legitimate, clearly stated purpose. Apply access controls, retention limits, security protections, and appropriate mechanisms for correction, deletion, and redress.

## Article 10 — Security and resilience

Security is an architectural requirement, not a marketing claim. Systems should be threat-modelled, tested, monitored, patched, and designed to fail safely. Critical safeguards should not depend on a single model output or a single point of failure.

## Article 11 — Constructive benefit

Priority should be given to applications that help people learn, access healthcare, discover and share knowledge, improve accessibility, protect ecosystems, maintain infrastructure, and meet essential needs—including food, water, energy, and shelter.

## Article 12 — Human agency and contestability

People affected by consequential automated decisions should, where appropriate and feasible, receive understandable information, access to meaningful human review, and a route to challenge errors or seek remedy.

## Article 13 — Independent evaluation

Claims of safety, fairness, robustness, or conformance must be supported by evidence. High-impact systems require proportionate independent assessment, adversarial testing, incident reporting, and reassessment after material changes.

## Article 14 — No self-amending exemption

A system must not be allowed to unilaterally remove its own binding safety constraints or expand its authority beyond approved limits. Changes to critical controls require authenticated authorization, review, and traceable records.

## Article 15 — Continuous correction

When serious risks, unexpected harms, or safeguard failures are identified, responsible parties should contain the issue, preserve relevant evidence, notify appropriate stakeholders as required, investigate root causes, and implement corrective action.

## Article 16 — Implementation and interpretation

This Protocol is a voluntary normative proposal unless adopted through a binding instrument, contract, policy, or other legally effective mechanism. It does not supersede applicable law, and it does not itself create legal rights, duties, certification, or immunity.

## Adoption statement

An organisation claiming alignment with Protocol 777 should publish the scope of its claim, identify accountable owners, document exceptions, and provide evidence against the [Conformance Checklist](CONFORMANCE_CHECKLIST.md). Alignment is not proof of absolute safety.
