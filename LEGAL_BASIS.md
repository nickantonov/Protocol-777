# Legal Basis and Reference Instruments

Protocol 777 draws on existing human-rights, humanitarian-law, and AI-governance frameworks. These instruments differ in legal status, scope, parties, and applicability. This document is a starting point for research, not a legal opinion.

## 1. Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)

The UDHR sets out a common standard of human rights and dignity. It is a declaration rather than a treaty, although many of its principles influence international and domestic law.

- Text: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights

## 2. International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)

The ICCPR is a treaty binding on its States Parties. Its protections include the right to life, liberty and security, privacy, freedom of thought and expression, equality, and effective remedies, subject to the wording and conditions of individual provisions.

- Text and status information: https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights

## 3. International humanitarian law and weapons reviews

Where international humanitarian law applies, relevant rules include distinction, proportionality, precautions in attack, and restrictions on means and methods of warfare. Additional Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions, Article 36, requires States Parties to determine whether study, development, acquisition, or adoption of a new weapon, means, or method of warfare would be prohibited in some or all circumstances by applicable international law. Its application depends on treaty status and the facts.

- ICRC overview of autonomous weapons: https://www.icrc.org/en/law-and-policy/autonomous-weapons
- ICRC position on autonomous weapon systems: https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-position-autonomous-weapon-systems

## 4. Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court

The Rome Statute defines the Court's jurisdiction over specified crimes, subject to its jurisdictional and admissibility rules. It is not a general AI statute, and it does not automatically make every harmful use of AI an international crime.

- Text: https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/Publications/Rome-Statute.pdf

## 5. Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law

This convention establishes a framework concerning AI activities and human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. Its binding effect for a State depends on signature, ratification, entry into force, and applicable reservations or declarations.

- Official information: https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence

## 6. European Union Artificial Intelligence Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 establishes a risk-based legal framework for AI in the EU, with obligations and application dates that differ by provision and actor. Whether a particular obligation applies must be checked against the current consolidated text, transitional rules, territorial scope, and relevant facts.

- Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj

## How these sources relate to Protocol 777

The Protocol's commitments are informed by these instruments, but they are not a verbatim restatement of any one of them. Some provisions are aspirational engineering recommendations; others restate broad ideas found in existing law. A specific system or deployment needs jurisdiction-specific legal analysis.

### Important limitations

- This repository is not legal advice and does not replace qualified counsel.
- The Protocol is not itself a treaty, statute, regulation, or certification standard.
- The legal status of each instrument must be verified for the relevant country and date.
- Humanitarian law generally applies in situations of armed conflict; it should not be assumed to govern every civilian AI deployment.
- A violation of an ethical principle is not automatically a violation of a particular law, and legal compliance alone does not prove that a system is ethically sound.
- References should be reviewed periodically for amendments, new interpretations, and changes in treaty status.
